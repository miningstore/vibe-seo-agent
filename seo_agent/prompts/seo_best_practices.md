# SEO best-practices rubric

Used by the variant generator when proposing copy for a slot. Each
proposal must satisfy these rules; the validator in `loop.py` enforces
the hard ones (length, banned tokens, JSON validity). The soft ones
are scored by the LLM as it composes the variant.

## Title tag (hard rules)

- 30–60 characters. Below 30 looks thin; above 60 truncates in SERP.
- Front-load the primary keyword (the noun the searcher will type).
  For city pages that is "{City} apartments" or "Average rent in {City}".
  For property pages it is the property name + "apartments".
- Include one quantified detail when it fits: median rent, unit count,
  building count, refresh recency. Numbers earn clicks because they
  pre-answer the searcher's implicit question.
- Avoid filler words ("Find", "Browse", "Explore", "Discover"). The
  searcher already knows they want to browse — the title should sell
  the *result*, not the verb.
- One pipe (`|`) max. Prefer it as a separator over hyphens or em dashes.
- Never use an em dash anywhere in the title. The site house style bans
  them; Google occasionally rewrites titles with em dashes.

## Meta description (hard rules)

- 70–160 characters. Below 70, Google rewrites it from on-page copy.
- Lead with the answer to the SERP question, not a tagline. For an
  apartments page that means: "X units in {City} from $Y/mo, updated
  daily." For a property page: "{Property} in {Neighborhood} — Z units
  from $W/mo, updated today."
- Include one concrete number (price, count, or date).
- One sentence is fine. Two is the maximum.
- No em dashes. No exclamation marks.

## H1 (hard rules)

- One H1 per page. Distinct from the title tag (different copy is fine;
  Google rewards lexical variation between title and H1).
- 20–80 characters. Front-load the searcher's keyword.
- Should answer "what is this page?" in a glance.

## Soft rules — quality signals

- **Specificity over generic phrasing.** "Average rent in Austin, TX" >
  "Apartments for rent in Austin." The first telegraphs a quantified
  market summary; the second is undifferentiated against 10k other pages.
- **Match search intent.** Listing pages target "{city} apartments for
  rent" (intent: browse). Property pages target "{property name}" +
  "{city} apartments" (intent: research a specific building). City index
  pages target "{city} rent" or "average rent {city}" (intent: research
  the market). Don't cross intents.
- **E-E-A-T signals (Google's quality framework).** Mentioning recency
  ("refreshed today", "as of {month}") and source-of-truth ("from each
  property's leasing system") raises trust signals. The site house style
  forbids naming vendor systems (Sightmap, RentCafe, Entrata, etc.) — say
  "each property's leasing system" instead.
- **Long-tail keyword inclusion.** When the GSC data shows a query getting
  impressions but no clicks (CTR < 1% with > 50 impressions), consider
  weaving that query phrase into the title or H1. That's "striking
  distance" optimization.
- **Numbers > adjectives.** "198 properties" > "many properties."
  "$1,847/mo" > "affordable rent." Numbers earn position 1–3 clicks
  because they pre-answer the question.

## House-style constraints (must enforce)

- **No em dashes** (–, —). Anywhere. Use periods, colons, or commas.
- **No vendor-system names** anywhere in user-facing copy. Banned tokens
  include: Sightmap, RentCafe, Entrata, Windsor, AMLI, Knock, Funnel,
  Appfolio, Hyly, Anyonehome, RealtyMX, Equity (when referring to the
  scraping target), Yardi. Comparing against *public* indexes (Zillow
  ZORI, RentCafe-as-third-party-index in research methodology only) is
  allowed inside research/methodology pages but NOT in user-facing slot
  copy generated by the optimizer.
- **No scraping vocabulary**: "scraped", "crawled", "harvested",
  "extracted", "data sourcing" is also too revealing — prefer "refreshed
  daily" or "pulled from each property's leasing system" or "current
  pricing."
- **No exaggerated claims**: "the best", "guaranteed", "always lowest",
  "exclusive" without basis. Use the data: "X units below $Y/mo today."
- **No fake numbers.** Every number in copy must trace to data we have.
  If the slot is a template with `{minPrice}`, the variant *also* uses
  that template; it does not invent a price.

## Schema.org slots (when the slot is a JSON-LD fragment)

- Must be valid JSON-LD with `@context: "https://schema.org"` and a
  legitimate `@type`.
- `AggregateOffer` for listing pages and city pages with priced units;
  `lowPrice`, `highPrice`, `priceCurrency`, `offerCount` should match
  the on-page data exactly (template substitution, not invention).
- `LocalBusiness` for property detail pages with `address`,
  `geo`, `aggregateRating` only when those values are present in the
  page data.
- `FAQPage` for FAQ-bearing pages. The `Question` text and `Answer`
  text MUST match the visible Q/A on the page (Google penalizes
  invisible FAQ markup).
- Never put a `priceRange` on a `LocalBusiness` because it's typically
  used for restaurant-style $$$ markers — wrong intent here.

## Variant generation guidance (soft)

When asked to propose N candidate treatments for a slot, prefer
diversity along these axes:

- **Tone**: utilitarian ("$1,847/mo average rent in Austin"), narrative
  ("Austin's rental market by the numbers"), question-led ("How much is
  rent in Austin?").
- **Number anchoring**: lead with median ("Median rent: $1,847/mo"),
  count ("198 priced apartments in Austin"), recency ("Today's Austin
  rents, refreshed 6am"), or value ("Compare 1,200 Austin units").
- **Length**: include a short variant (~35 chars) and a richer one
  (~58 chars) so the bandit can learn which length wins per page family.

Do NOT include emojis, Unicode arrows, or special punctuation.
